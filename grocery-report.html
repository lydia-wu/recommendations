Why does October look high?

It is only a little above your usual month on the receipts alone (+$23, +5%). It feels high because several things that usually soften a month were missing:

  1. No family treats. Treats covered $171 in May and $342 in September, and nothing in October. After treats, a usual month is $449 and October was $529.
  2. No savings logged. Coupons, rewards and credits usually take about $15 off a month; October had $0.
  3. Fewer costs pushed into later months. Only $77 of October purchases was spread forward, versus $154 in a usual month. About $130 of October's total came from earlier purchases.
  4. More shopping trips: 16 versus 14 in a usual month.
  5. Category mix. Up: pantry +$33, dairy +$19, veggies +$19, drinks +$11. Offsetting: fruits −$24 and meat −$34.

Too much in one category? No single category stands out across the year. The October bump is mostly pantry & spices (fresh that-day honey 1 quart, pure maple syrup, coconut milk, apple butter 1 pint, classic mayo), then dairy and veggies.

How this was calculated

  • Each purchase is split evenly over the months in column 6 (starting in the purchase month, or the override month in column 8). Treats are counted on the day they were logged.
  • Coupons, member savings, store rewards, card points and credits are subtracted from the month they landed in, spread across categories by share. Discounts on a specific item stay with that item's category.
  • Left out: Walmart "temporary hold" lines and their refunds, plus non-food items (hand sanitizer, nasal spray, flowers). Taxes, bag and delivery fees and tips are in "Tax, fees & tips".
  • Bourbon, prosecco and beer are in Drinks & alcohol. Extracts, vinegar, spices and baking staples are in Pantry & spices.
  • November so far ($128) and about $72 already allocated to December are not part of the averages.
