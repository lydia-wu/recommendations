A few critical notes before diving in

The Thanksgiving week problem. November 21 is the Saturday before Thanksgiving — one of the most expensive flying weekends of the year. Southwest's low-fare calendar shows November 2026 starting from $149 one-way BWI→FLL, but the actual Nov 21/28 dates will likely run $175–$275 per person one-way. Book flights early. Alternatively, the November 28 sailing (Option A also has a Nov 28 date) departs the Saturday after Thanksgiving, when flights return from Fort Lauderdale to DC on Dec 5 — those return flights are typically much cheaper.

Gratuities are not included in either cruise fare. Holland America charges about $18 per person per day; Princess charges about $16. For 7 nights, that's $252 (HAL) or $224 (Princess) for two people — budget for this.

The Ships and Why They Fit

Option A: Holland America Eurodam — the stronger lifestyle match

Holland America is the cruise line most naturally aligned with this couple. HAL's entire brand identity is built around older, quieter travelers who don't drink heavily, aren't interested in waterparks or nightclubs, and want to actually experience the ports rather than the ship. A few specific reasons it fits:

The Eurodam itself is a mid-sized Signature Class ship carrying about 2,100 passengers — small enough to feel personal, large enough to have excellent amenities. It was refurbished in 2023. The ship features the World Stage theater, BBC Earth in Concert programming, a Greenhouse Spa & Salon, and the Pinnacle Grill steakhouse. The Music Walk concept offers live music across multiple venues (Lincoln Center Stage for classical, Billboard Onboard for pop, B.B. King Blues Club for jazz and blues) — which would appeal to the couple's love of concert band and live performance. This is not a rowdy ship; the vibe is deliberately calm and cultivated.

Holland America's "EXC" programming (Explorations Central) is built specifically for curious, intellectually engaged guests. This includes onboard naturalist talks, port lectures, hands-on culinary demonstrations at the Culinary Arts Center, and documentary film screenings. A couple who loves museums, nature, and detail-oriented learning will find HAL's onboard rhythm genuinely satisfying during sea days.

The Culinary Arts Center deserves special mention: it runs hands-on cooking classes and demonstrations — this fits beautifully with a couple who loves making things with their hands (knitting, embroidery, carpentry).

Key West as a port is an excellent fit. It's compact, walkable, quiet by cruise-ship port standards, full of history and quirky museums (Ernest Hemingway's home and six-toed cats, the Key West Butterfly Conservatory, the Key West Aquarium), and easy to navigate independently. It's the kind of place where two people in their early 50s can wander at their own pace without a guide.

Half Moon Cay (now renamed "RelaxAway, Half Moon Cay") is HAL's private island in the Bahamas. As part of the 2026-2027 season, guests will be able to enjoy enhancements at the destination, including a new beach club featuring priority tendering, waiter service and exclusive food and beverage offerings. The snorkeling there is genuinely excellent — clear, calm, shallow-water reef snorkeling is exactly what someone who "loved snorkeling in the past" would enjoy getting back into after years away. It's low-stakes and beautiful.

One important flag: November 21 is the Saturday before Thanksgiving. HAL's holiday cruises sometimes include extra festive programming, which can bring aboard more families. The Dec 5 sailing of the same itinerary would be quieter and typically cheaper.
